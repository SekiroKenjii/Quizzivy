# R9 — Insights (v0.15.0)

The release that turns results into grades. A teacher gets the deck's Gradebook (a class's
weighted grades for a term, with CSV and Excel export) and Reports (four KPIs with deltas, a
weekly average, skills and a watch list). A student gets Grades. The wizard's Schedule step
gains the assignment's gradebook category and weight (DG-93). The query review Phase 5 planned
(T-5.5) is re-homed here, because R9 adds the heaviest read queries in the product and they
should be measured at volume before v1.0.

R9 runs after R8 (`78-r8.md`: terms, sessions and attendance), because "this term" and the
Attendance KPI read R8's tables (70 §2 rule 7). It also reads R5's activity tracking
(`75-r5.md`) for "Active students" and "Has never signed in", R4's frozen question `skill`
(`74-r4.md` T-R4.15) for skills, R4's review options (T-R4.11) for what a student may see, and
R7's Messages (`77-r7.md`) for the watch list's Message button. Screens are compared with
`docs/design/deck/Quizzivy Teacher.dc.html` (`r.gradebook`, `r.reports`) and
`Quizzivy Student.dc.html` (`grades`). Branch `work/redesign-r9` is cut from `develop`.

**Deliverable:** the assignment's gradebook category and weight, set in the wizard and on the
assignment's Settings tab; the Gradebook per class and term, with bands, the legend, a pager and
a formula-safe CSV/Excel export; Reports whose class and range selectors drive the data (DG-70);
the student's Grades page and nav tab; a volume seed, `EXPLAIN` captures and p95 budgets for
every heavy read.

**Exit criteria:**

- Gradebook, Reports and Grades match the deck at 360, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440, light and dark,
  with data shaped like the deck's fixtures (T-R9.13).
- R2's isolation suite (T-R2.16) covers every new operation, each query `classId` and `termId`
  declared per parameter: its two-teacher pair shows another teacher's `classId` answering 404
  on the gradebook, its export and Reports, and its two-student pair does the same on
  `/app/grades`. A student sees only their own results, and never a score the review policy
  withholds.
- Every export cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or CR is escaped, in CSV and XLSX.
- The volume seed exists, `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` for every query T-R9.11 covers is committed
  under `docs/perf/`, and the p95 budgets in T-R9.11 pass at volume.
- The five canaries pass untouched; every CI step of 70 §8 is green locally.
- Released to production as `v0.15.0`.

**Decisions taken in this file, worth a second look.**

- **The per-assignment weight is the source of truth; category percentages are derived.** The
  deck's header reads "tests 60%, homework 40%", and its columns carry 10, 10, 20, 25, 15 and 20,
  which sum to exactly those totals. Storing both would let them disagree. An assignment stores
  `gradebook_category` (test or homework) and `gradebook_weight` (0–100). The header shows each
  category's share of the term's total weight. A weight of 0 keeps a column visible and out of
  the Final.
- **Existing assignments become "Test, weight 10" through constant column defaults.** Equal
  weights reproduce a plain average, which is the least surprising grade for work assigned before
  weights existed. A constant default is written into the catalogue without a table rewrite, and
  the R8 binary's inserts receive it during the rolling deploy, so no fill trigger is needed (70
  §3). An update that omits `gradebook` keeps the stored values, so an R8 tab cannot reset a
  weight.
- **A cell is the student's best graded attempt.** "The best score counts" is the wizard's
  promise for multi-attempt assignments (R4), and R4's class average uses the same rule. A cell is
  Pending only when nothing is graded yet and an attempt awaits grading. A graded 70% beside a
  second attempt still waiting shows 70%, with the waiting attempt in the tooltip, because a known
  number is more useful to the Final than a hole. Voided attempts never count. An attempt still in
  progress counts as not submitted.
- **Missing and pending cells are left out of the Final, as the deck states.** The deck computes
  the Final as the weighted mean over numeric cells only, renormalised. A student who skipped a
  closed test is therefore not pulled down by it. That rule is the deck's, and the Reports watch
  list ("Missed 2 assignments") is where a skipped test surfaces. Whether a missing cell should
  count as zero once the window has closed is O-R9.1.
- **A column belongs to the term that contains its assignment's `closes_at`.** Terms come from R8.
  The gradebook opens on the current term (the one containing today, else the most recent past
  term). With no terms defined it shows every assignment, and the header says "All terms".
  Weights are renormalised per term, so a term's Final never depends on another term's work.
- **Gradebook rows are the class's current, active members; columns are the assignments that
  target the class.** An assignment given to individual students is not a class column, and a
  draft is not a column at all. A student removed from the class leaves its gradebook, and their
  results stay on the assignment detail, as R4's "Remove from class" promises. Disabled students
  leave every denominator (NFR-R07).
- **Reports' definitions** (all in the teacher's time zone, over the selected classes):
  - *Average score*: the mean percentage of counted attempts (best graded per student and
    assignment) submitted in the range. The delta is in points against the previous period of
    the same length.
  - *Completion rate*: of the (student, assignment) pairs whose effective close (R4's per-student
    overrides included) falls in the range, the share handed in before that close.
  - *Active students*: active students with an activity day (R5) in the last seven days, over all
    active students in the classes. It ignores the range selector, because the deck's hint is
    "Signed in this week" and activity is a current state, not a history.
  - *Attendance*: R8's roster rate (present and late over marked), over the sessions in range,
    with the session count as the hint. The KPI shows "—" when no attendance was taken.
  - *This term* compares with the previous term, and hides the delta when there is none.
- **The watch list shows one reason per student, the most urgent first**, capped at ten rows:
  average dropped by 10 points or more (at least two counted attempts in each period), then two or
  more flagged attempts in the range (D8), then two or more missed assignments in the range, then
  "Has never signed in". The deck draws one chip per row; a student who meets several reasons is
  listed under the first.
- **Reports keeps the deck's range options: 4 weeks, 8 weeks, 12 weeks, This term.** DG-70's
  interim "7 days, 30 days, this term" was written against the dashboard, which R4 built as
  7/14/30 days. Both selectors now drive their queries, as DG-70 asks.
- **The student's Grades page never receives a score the review policy withholds.** A row shows
  the score only when the student's result page would (R4's `review_show_score` and
  `review_release`). The Average and skill tiles use those rows only. An average that included
  withheld scores would leak them by subtraction. A row whose score is withheld holds the latest
  handed-in attempt, not the best graded one, because picking the best would reveal which
  attempt scored higher.
- **The gradebook export runs in the API, bounded, not in the worker.** 70 moves unbounded jobs
  (R5's data export, user import with an argon2 hash per row) to the worker process group. A
  gradebook export is one teacher's classes for one term: a few dozen columns by a few hundred
  rows, with no hashing. It streams CSV through `encoding/csv`, and XLSX through excelize's
  `StreamWriter`, which flushes rows to a temp file instead of holding the sheet in memory. Hard
  caps (40 classes, 200 columns per class, 20,000 cells per class) answer
  `422 EXPORT_TOO_LARGE` with the per-class export as the way out.
- **"All classes" as CSV is a ZIP with one CSV per class; as Excel it is one sheet per class.**
  Classes have different columns, so a single CSV would need either a long format no teacher
  expects or a union of columns that is mostly empty.
- **One `gradebook` module holds the three read models (gradebook, reports, student grades).**
  All three read the same tables with the same "counted attempt" rule, and a second module
  would have to duplicate it. Like `dashboard`, the module reads other modules' tables and writes nothing. Weights are
  written by the assignments module.
- **Nothing on these screens polls.** Each refetches on focus and after a weight change. A
  gradebook left open on a teacher's second monitor must not keep Neon awake (PR-7).

### T-R9.2 — Gradebook read model and API
**Depends on:** T-R9.1
**Touches:** `server/internal/modules/gradebook/` (new: `domain/{gradebook,final,repository}.go`, `application/{app.go,query/gradebook.go}`, `repositories/gradebook.go`, `http/gradebook.go`), `server/internal/core/wiring/gradebook.go` (new), `server/internal/core/router/{modules.go,server.go}`, `api/openapi.yaml` (tag `teacher-gradebook`), generated code
**Size:** L
**Done when:**
- [ ] The package comment names the model: read models over results, never written through.
- [ ] `GET /teacher/gradebook?classId=&termId=&page=&limit=` (`x-permission: teaching.grading`)
      returns:
      - `classes: [{id, name}]`, the tabs: the caller's classes under R2's class scope,
        archived classes excluded (an archived `classId` still answers, read-only)
      - `class`, `term: {id, name, startsOn, endsOn} | null` and `terms: [{id, name}]`
      - `categoryShares: {test, homework}` as percentages of the term's total weight
      - `columns: [{assignmentId, title, category, weight, closesAt}]`, ordered by `closesAt`
      - `rows: [{student: {id, fullName}, cells: [...], final: number | null}]`, where a cell is
        `{assignmentId, state: graded|pending|missing, percent, score, max, attemptId,
        submittedAt, awaitingGrading}`, with the numeric fields null unless graded
      - `page`, `limit` (default 25, maximum 100, per O-20) and `total`
- [ ] Without `classId`, the first class tab is used. Without `termId`, the current term is used,
      else the latest past term, else all assignments.
- [ ] `domain/final.go` holds the one rule: the weighted mean of graded cells, renormalised over
      their weights, rounded to one decimal; null when no graded cell has a weight above 0. Bands
      are a web concern.
- [ ] The counted attempt is chosen in SQL: the maximum of `score_earned / score_total` over
      attempts with `graded_at` set and status not `voided`, per student and assignment. Pending
      means a `submitted` or `timed_out` attempt without `graded_at`. The query never orders by
      `final_score` (AGENTS: a virtual column).
- [ ] The query count is fixed: one statement for columns, one for the page of rows and cells, and
      one for the total, whatever the class size (a pgx tracer counts them in the test).
- [ ] Another teacher's `classId` answers 404. The operation joins R2's two-teacher isolation
      suite with `x-resource: [{in: query, name: classId, kind: class}, {in: query, name:
      termId, kind: none}]` (T-R2.16's per-parameter form; terms are organisation-wide, not
      owned).
- [ ] Tests:
      - unit: `final_test.go` table (renormalisation, weight 0, all pending, all missing, one
        decimal)
      - integration: best-of-attempts, voided ignored, pending beside graded, term boundaries, no
        terms, archived class, a removed member absent, a disabled member absent
      - contract: the response validates against the schema

### T-R9.3 — Gradebook export: CSV and Excel, formula-safe

**Size:** M
**Done when:**
- [ ] `GET /teacher/gradebook/export?format=csv|xlsx&scope=class|all&classId=&termId=&comments=`
      (`x-permission: teaching.grading`). It streams with `Content-Disposition: attachment`:
      - `format=csv`, class: `text/csv; charset=utf-8` with a BOM
      - `format=csv`, all: `application/zip` holding one CSV per class

        sheet per class
- [ ] Columns: Student, Email, then per assessment "{title} ({category} · {weight}%)", then

      counted attempt's `teacher_note`. The export is teacher-only, and the note is the teacher's
      own.
- [ ] Cell values follow the screen: a graded percentage, "Pending", or empty. In XLSX,
      percentages are numeric cells and never strings.

- [ ] `RateLimits()` gains `GET /teacher/gradebook/export` at 10 a minute per IP. It is a heavy

- [ ] `x-resource: [{in: query, name: classId, kind: class}, {in: query, name: termId, kind:
