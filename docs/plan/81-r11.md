# R11 — Landing, leads and v1.0 hardening (v1.0.0)

The last release of Phase R. It puts the deck's Landing on the apex `quizzivy.com` (D10), makes
the apex a second origin of the same app, moves join links and QR codes to it, and adds the
consultation form with consent, a privacy notice and an Admin list of requests (D18, DG-06,
DG-57). It then runs the hardening Phase 5 planned and never shipped: the rate-limit suite and
leak review (T-5.2), the accessibility pass (T-5.3), bundle budgets (T-5.4) and the native
Vietnamese review (T-5.8). The query review (T-5.5) shipped in R9. The Landing ships last
because its copy markets every module (70 §2 rule 8).

Screens are compared with `docs/design/deck/Quizzivy Landing.dc.html`. The privacy, status and
Admin consultation pages are not drawn and are built from deck parts (DG-06, DG-57, DG-100).
Branch `work/redesign-r11` is cut from `develop`. External steps (Cloudflare, Google console) are
Thuong's, as in T-R0.8.

**Deliverable:** the Landing at `https://quizzivy.com/` in Vietnamese and English, light and
dark, with join by code and a consultation form; the apex as a second app origin (credentialed
CORS, a session hint cookie, a second Google redirect URI); join links and QR codes on the apex;
leads stored with consent for 12 months, emailed to the centre without the phone number, and
listed in the Admin console; a privacy notice and a status page; the rate-limit suite, axe in CI,
bundle budgets, the reviewed Vietnamese copy; the spec at v1.0.

**Exit criteria:**

- The Landing matches the deck at 360, 720, 768, 960, 1024, 1280 and 1440, light and dark, vi and
  en (T-R11.15).
- An anonymous visit to `https://quizzivy.com/` makes no request to the API. A signed-in visit
  lands on the visitor's console. Sign-in, Google sign-in and join work on both origins
  (`apex.live.spec.ts`).
- Every open operation is driven past its limit by the suite, and the leak review of every public
  response is recorded.
- axe reports no serious or critical violation on the routes of T-R11.11. Lighthouse
  accessibility is 95 or more on the eight routes named there.
- Every route's initial JS is within `web/bundle-budgets.json`, and the Landing's is under
  200 KB gzip.
- The native review is applied, and vi and en have the same keys.
- The five canaries pass untouched; every CI step of 70 §8 is green locally, including
  `pnpm e2e:live` and `pnpm e2e:live-apex`.
- Released to production as `v1.0.0`.

**Order of work.** The hint cookie (T-R11.1) and the leads backend (T-R11.6) come first, and the
locale split (T-R11.12a) early, because every later landing string lands in its file. The root
route (T-R11.3) needs T-R11.1. The landing sections, the form and the pages follow. The apex
infrastructure (T-R11.2) is prepared in parallel and switched on at release. The hardening tasks
(T-R11.10–T-R11.13) run once the product is feature-complete. At most four agents at a time.

**Decisions taken in this file, worth a second look.**

- **The apex serves the same SPA as `app.quizzivy.com`, and differs only at `/`.** On the host
  named by `VITE_LANDING_HOST` (`quizzivy.com`), `/` is the Landing. On the app host, `/` keeps
  today's redirect to the console or to `/login`. Every other route is identical on both. The
  Landing's links (`/login`, `/join/:code`, `/privacy`) therefore stay on the apex, and a student
  who opens a join QR finishes signing in and enrolling on the origin they started on.
- **A signed-in visitor on the apex stays on the apex.** Bouncing them to `app.quizzivy.com`
  would drop the in-memory access token mid-flow and split the join journey across two origins.
  `localStorage` is per origin, so theme, language and local answer drafts are kept separately on
  each; drafts are backed by server autosave, so nothing is lost. Console pages carry
  `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on both hosts.
- **"Signed in?" comes from a non-secret hint cookie, never from a refresh probe.** The API sets
  `quizzivy_signed_in=1; Domain=quizzivy.com; Path=/; Secure; SameSite=Lax` (not `HttpOnly`,
  holding no identity), with the refresh cookie's lifetime, whenever it sets the refresh cookie.
  It clears the hint on logout and on every refused refresh. The Landing calls `/auth/refresh`
  only when the hint is present. Probing on every visit would wake Neon for every anonymous
  visitor and crawler, and rotate tokens against open app tabs. A `localStorage` flag cannot work,
  because it is per origin. The hint is a performance hint, never an authorisation signal: the
  refresh cookie still decides.
- **The cross-origin refresh race is the residual R-06 already accepts.** Two tabs on different
  origins refreshing at the same moment can trip reuse detection, exactly as two tabs on one
  origin can today. The hint keeps anonymous visits off `/auth/refresh`, so the apex adds no new
  race for people who are not signed in. A server grace window would be a §5 change and is not
  made here.
- **An anonymous Landing makes no API call at all.** The contact block (phone, email, address)
  reads build-time `VITE_ORG_*` values, not R5's organization settings: R1's `VITE_ORG_NAME` and
  `VITE_ORG_FRONT_DESK_PHONE`, and `VITE_ORG_EMAIL` and `VITE_ORG_ADDRESS`, which T-R11.4b adds.
  R5 keeps these build values as the Landing's source and the front door's fallback. A public
  read of those settings would reach the database on every visit, and a crawler would keep Neon
  awake. The cost is that editing the contact details in Admin settings reaches the Landing at
  the next build (O-R11.4). The consultation submit and the `/status` page are the only calls,
  each on an explicit action.
- **The app host keeps probing the session without a hint.** Users signed in before v1.0 hold a
  refresh cookie but no hint until their next refresh. Gating the app host on the hint would sign
  them all out. Only the Landing route is gated.
- **Join links and QR codes move to the apex; nothing else does.** `VITE_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`
  (`https://quizzivy.com`) builds the class link and the QR payload. Old `app.quizzivy.com/join/…`
  links and printed QR codes keep working, because the route exists on both hosts. Email links
  (R5's set-password link, R7's notifications) stay on the app host, where the consoles have
  always lived.
- **Testimonials render only with recorded consent, and never as placeholders.** DG-100's interim
  is "placeholder testimonials until consent". On a production page, a quote with a name that
  nobody said is a fabricated review. The section reads a static list whose entries each carry a
  `consentRecordedAt`, and it is hidden while the list is empty (O-R11.2).
- **Marketing copy states what shipped.** The deck's copy predates several releases. Features says
  "Five question types", where R6 shipped more. FAQ 1 says students "create an account", but only
  Google sign-in creates one. FAQ 5 describes a reset flow that R5 changed. The product mock's
  address pill reads `quizzivy.com/admin` for a teacher dashboard. Each line is rewritten to match
  the product (vi first), and the PR lists every sentence that departs from the deck.
- **A lead submission always answers `202 {received: true}`.** Nothing is echoed. Validation
  names only the field. Per-IP limits answer 429. Two cases are dropped silently and still answer
  202: a honeypot field that was filled, and a phone number with three requests in the last
  24 hours. A 429 there would tell anyone that a number had been used. There is no captcha: it
  would add a third party and bot detection to a form with a few requests a day.
- **Consent is part of the contract.** The request must carry `consent: true` and a
  `privacyNoticeVersion` from an enum (the first is `2026-10`), so the schema refuses anything
  else. Both are stored with the lead. Changing the notice adds an enum value, and the stored
  version says what each person agreed to.
- **The notification email carries no phone number.** It gives the first name, the interests, who
  the course is for, the best time to call, and a link to `/admin/leads/{id}`, which needs an
  admin session. The person agreed to a call back, not to their number sitting in several
  inboxes (the same rule as R5's data-export link and D15).
- **A lead stores no IP address or user agent.** The rate limiter already holds the IP, in memory
  only. The lead holds what the form asked for, the consent, and its handling. The retention
  sweep deletes leads after 12 months (D18), and an admin can delete one on request.
- **Consultation requests sit under the Admin console's People group**, as "Consultation
  requests", behind the hidden `system.leads` key (70 §4.1), with a count badge of new requests
  like the Users badge. DG-57 names no place for them. People is where the Admin console lists
  the other humans it tracks.
- **Locale files split by surface, keys unchanged.** Today both locales (about 260 KB of JSON)
  sit in the entry chunk. That alone would break any honest Landing budget. The files split by
  top-level key into `common`, `auth`, `landing`, `student`, `teacher` and `admin`. `common` stays
  in the entry, and each tree loads its surface for the active language before rendering, merged
  into the one `translation` namespace. No `t()` call site changes, and the copy-pinned tests load
  every surface in setup. The split is its own mechanical PR.
- **Budgets are measured, then pinned; the Landing's has a ceiling.** Each route's budget is its
  R11 measurement plus 10%, in `web/bundle-budgets.json`. Raising a budget is a reviewed change to
  that file. The Landing may not exceed 200 KB gzip whatever the measurement, because it is the
  page a stranger opens cold on a phone.
- **The rate-limit suite is a Go end-to-end test over the contract, not a Playwright spec.**
  Limits are server behaviour. The suite reads `api/openapi.yaml`, so a new open operation without
  a case fails the build. The web's 429 states are unit-tested with the screens that show them.
- **`/status` is a real page.** The deck's footer links "Status" to the maintenance frame (DG-100).
  `/status` reads `GET /public/status` (R1) when visited, and says "All systems normal", shows the
  scheduled window, or shows R1's maintenance content.

### T-R11.1 — The session hint cookie
**Depends on:** —
**Touches:** `server/internal/modules/identity/http/{cookie.go,auth.go,google.go}`, R5's set-password completion handler, `server/internal/platform/config/config.go`, `server/internal/platform/config/tests/`, `server/internal/modules/identity/http/tests/cookie_test.go`, `api/openapi.yaml` (the `Set-Cookie` descriptions on login, Google sign-in, refresh, logout and set-password), generated code, `.env.example`, `fly.toml`
**Size:** S
**Done when:**
- [ ] Config `SESSION_HINT_COOKIE_DOMAIN`: a bare domain (no scheme, port or path; startup refuses
      anything else). Empty means a host-only cookie, which is what local development on
      `localhost` needs. `.env.example` documents it, and `fly.toml` sets `quizzivy.com`.
- [ ] `sessionHintCookie` builds `quizzivy_signed_in=1` with `Path=/`, the configured `Domain`,
      `Secure` from `REFRESH_COOKIE_SECURE`, `SameSite=Lax`, no `HttpOnly`, and `Max-Age` equal
      to the refresh TTL. `clearSessionHintCookie` sets the same attributes with `Max-Age=-1`.
- [ ] Every response that sets the refresh cookie also sets the hint: login, Google sign-in,
      refresh, and R5's set-password completion when it signs the user in. Logout clears both.
      Every refused refresh (401) clears the hint.
- [ ] Two cookies need two `Set-Cookie` headers, which the generated single-header response types
      cannot write. Those responses use visitors that add both, as `clearedSession` does for
      logout today.
- [ ] Tests: the attributes; set on each path; cleared on logout and on each refusal outcome
      (unknown, expired, reused, revoked, disabled); host-only when the domain is empty; config
      refusal.
- [ ] `client.refresh.test.ts` is untouched. No web code reads the hint before T-R11.3.
